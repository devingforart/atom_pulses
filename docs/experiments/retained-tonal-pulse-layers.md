# Persistent complementary tonal-rhythm layers

Reference intent: the listener's Desktop/intensity_rythms.mid has one pitched
lane, one pitch, short gates and a substantially retained two-bar identity over
16 bars. The engine does not read, import or tile those notes. The reference
motivates a production function: several complementary, persistent short-note
tonal layers, not another pair of melodic speakers.

Club cast planning now explicitly considers this fabric alongside bass, actual
chord stabs, sustained harmony and foreground. Each AI-selected dedicated owner
declares `retained_tonal_pulse` in its immutable functional role. Small explicit
casts and user exclusions take precedence; no host appends tracks or imposes
an automatic instrument-count quota.

The current cast brief targets at least four dedicated pulse owners, normally
four or five, in a full Club arrangement. Explicit smaller casts and exclusions
still take precedence. Bass, chord stabs and the sustained bed do not count as
these dedicated owners. This is AI arrangement direction, not a new rejection
quota or a host-generated extra track. Planning and writing both request a
complementary mixture of rapid grouped attacks, supporting gaps and accents,
with restrained dynamic/articulation variations and stable identities. No
reference onsets, fixed attack counts or velocity curves are supplied.

That declared role follows the existing functional-contract pipeline. Pulse
owners are excluded from melodic handoffs and constrained to monophonic,
cycle-bound static identities with one retained pitch chosen by the AI inside
its register/tonal policy. Each must have its own playable nucleus reference.
Their reference, actual writing and activity remain AI-authored. Inconsistency
is rejected or routed to existing scoped AI reference repair, never retuned.

The AI chooses each pulse's offsets, gaps, articulation, accents, recurrence and
sectional retention/withdrawal. A grouped pulse is not forced to become the
uniform meter carrier; the existing low anchor may supply that reference.
These roles are production decisions, not fabricated verbatim user requests.
Schema constraints reuse existing monophonic/repeat codecs without duplicating
required fields when a pulse was also explicitly requested by the user.

`tonal_pulse_owners` in the accepted phrase plan records the derived owner IDs
for final MIDI audit. A constant pitch does not prove sufficient rhythmic body,
nor does metadata prove a convincing result: inspect actual attack patterns,
retention, dynamics, interactions and listener response after generation. This
experiment changes only local engine sources; no Cloud deployment or promotion.

## Typed chord/pulse distinction

New Club cast manifests declare `tonal_rhythm_kind` per owner: `none`,
`single_tone_pulse` or `chord_stabs`. This one structured AI decision takes
precedence over the older descriptive role tag. Detail shards cannot lose the
classification: the host copies the exact accepted kind when merging details.
Single-tone owners keep the one-anchor contract; chord-stab owners instead keep
polyphony and actual chord attacks, with harmonic variants allowed as authored.
Neither a track's name nor a hardcoded list chooses the kind.

Legacy blueprints with ambiguous tags are clarified before planning notes. An
accepted explicit attack structure is mapped losslessly and locked; otherwise
one bounded AI metadata-only call chooses the missing kinds. Only those fields
may change. Cast IDs, role text, activity, harmony, form and existing notes are
not rewritten by the adapter. The clarified blueprint is stored in the new job,
not written over the archived source. There is no silent exception for a
particular track or permission to bypass scale/MIDI validation.

## Required playable references and complete acceptance

The failed `22da97d1-c0df-4c70-818d-8792c0b38593` response retained
`pulse_air`'s monophonic, single-anchor cycle identity but omitted its nucleus
lane. The older lane array required groove-core references, not every dedicated
pulse; non-core owners could therefore be forgotten. Focused plan repair also
accepted general architecture validity without checking functional allocation
until after leaving its repair loop.

New Club response schemas require `retained_pulse_references`, an owner-keyed
map containing notes and repeat intent for every cast-declared pulse. Generic
nucleus lanes exclude those IDs, preventing double declarations. Decoding only
maps the owner keys to canonical `instrument_id` fields and preserves all
AI-authored note values. It never creates a rhythm or tiles a reference. Missing,
duplicate or extra references are rejected atomically; old canonical plans
remain readable. Reference capacity follows the cast instead of a fixed twelve
owners.

Every successful focused repair now passes both architecture and functional
allocation validation before acceptance. For historical plans, a missing pulse
reference is diagnosed separately from a broken identity and routed to one
owner's AI note repair. Only cast-declared pulses (or existing groove-core
owners) may receive a missing lane; this does not make every pulse a core
protagonist. Its AI-chosen anchor remains enforced, and all other lanes stay
unchanged. These references are planning evidence, not automatically rendered
or copied into the final MIDI.
